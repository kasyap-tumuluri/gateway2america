Who we are

A free service, run by volunteers, for people the system was not designed for

Gateway2America has been active since October 2024. We're not a law firm or a government agency, and there's no business sitting behind any of this either. We answer questions about the road to U.S. citizenship mostly because somebody ought to, and because nobody should have to pay to have the basics explained to them.

Why we started

Mostly, people just get lost

When somebody stalls out on the road to citizenship, eligibility usually turns out to have very little to do with it. They've simply lost the thread somewhere along the way.

Part of it is that the forms are named instead of described. Nothing about "N-400" tells you it's the citizenship application, and nothing about "I-90" hints that it renews your green card. The official guidance is accurate and genuinely thorough, to be fair, but it's written at a reading level that assumes an education plenty of people never got the chance to have, usually in a language that isn't their first.

The other part is a gap right in the middle of the help that already exists. If your situation is genuinely complicated you need an attorney, and there are good nonprofits who provide that for free. A huge number of questions aren't complicated at all, though. They're just one question. Does the trip I took last year reset my clock? Which form am I actually supposed to use? Is nine months of silence from USCIS normal? Questions like that are too small to justify booking a legal appointment and too specific for a search engine to get right, and guessing wrong on them can cost you months or thousands of dollars.

That gap is where we work. Legal advice is genuinely beyond us, and we won't pretend otherwise, but reading the official source, working out what it means, and putting it into ordinary words within a day or two is something we can do — and then we hand you the link so you can check our work.

How well you can navigate immigration bureaucracy depends an awful lot on your English, your education, your money, and whether you happen to know somebody who's been through it already. Even with all of those going for you the system is difficult, and without them it tips over into something close to impossible. We're here to close some part of that gap.

How we work

  • Everything is free

    There's nothing to buy here, no premium tier, no referral commission — basically no financial interest in your case whatsoever.

  • We cite our sources

    Every policy article links back to the official notice, or to the organization that analysed it, so you never have to take our word for anything.

  • We say when we don't know

    If your question really needs a lawyer, we'll tell you that plainly and point you toward free options instead of guessing, since a confident wrong answer tends to do more damage than no answer at all.

  • We hold nothing

    We never ask for your name or your date of birth, nothing gets stored on this website, and your message goes in the bin within seven days of us helping you.

Founder

Ronit Luthra

Portrait to be added

Ronit Luthra founded Gateway2America in October 2024 and still answers most of the inquiries that come in.

The whole thing grew out of a fairly simple observation. People around him who were working toward citizenship kept getting stuck on things that did have clear, findable answers somewhere, just never answers written with them in mind. A form edition that had quietly changed. A trip abroad nobody had warned them to document. A notice with the real deadline buried halfway down a paragraph of procedural language.

What started as helping family and neighbours read their mail turned into something a bit more organized — somewhere you could send a question and get a real answer from an actual person, without paying for it and without handing over your personal details to do it. More than a hundred inquiries later, the shape of the work hasn't really changed. Someone writes in without much idea of where to start, and a day or two later they have an answer in plain language with a link to the official source attached.

In 2026, that work was recognised with a Princeton Prize in Race Relations Certificate of Accomplishment for the Eastern Pennsylvania and Delaware region, and with a Certificate of Special Congressional Recognition from Pennsylvania's 6th congressional district.
