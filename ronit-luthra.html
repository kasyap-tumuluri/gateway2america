Ronit Luthra

Ronit Luthra founded Gateway2America in October 2024 and still answers most of the inquiries that come in. The work has been recognised with a Princeton Prize in Race Relations certificate and a Certificate of Special Congressional Recognition from Pennsylvania's 6th district.

Why he started it

The whole thing grew out of a fairly ordinary observation. People around him who were working toward citizenship kept getting stuck on things that did have clear answers somewhere, just never answers written with them in mind. A form edition that had quietly changed. A trip abroad nobody had warned them to document. A notice with the real deadline buried halfway down a paragraph of procedural language.

None of those are hard problems if you already know how the system is put together. They are close to impossible if you do not, and the official guidance, accurate as it is, tends to be written at a reading level that assumes an education plenty of people never had the chance to get, usually in a language that is not their first.

So what began as helping family and neighbours read their mail turned into something more organized. More than a hundred inquiries later, the shape of the work has not changed much: somebody writes in without much idea where to start, and a day or two later they have an answer in plain language with a link to the official source attached.

How well you can navigate immigration bureaucracy depends an awful lot on your English, your education, your money, and whether you happen to know somebody who has been through it already. Even with all of those going for you the system is difficult. Without them it tips over into something close to impossible.

What the work actually involves

Reading an official source, working out what it means, and writing it back in ordinary words, usually within a day or two. Tutoring, free, for the civics and English parts of the naturalization test. Keeping a running record of policy changes that genuinely affect people, each one linked to the notice it came from so anyone can check it.

Legal advice is not part of it and never has been. When a question needs an attorney, the answer is to say so plainly and point toward free and low-cost options rather than guess.
