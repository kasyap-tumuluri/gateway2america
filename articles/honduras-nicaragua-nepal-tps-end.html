TPS ends for Honduras, Nicaragua, and Nepal

September 11, 2025Policy Update

Three Temporary Protected Status designations ended within weeks of each other: Nepal on August 20, 2025, and Honduras and Nicaragua on September 8, 2025.

Honduras and Nicaragua had been designated since 1999. Many beneficiaries had lived in the United States for more than 25 years, raising U.S. citizen children and building careers on a status that was always labelled temporary.

The most overlooked option

If you have a U.S. citizen child who is now 21 or older, that child can petition for you as a parent. For people who have held TPS since 1999, children born here in the early years of that status are now well past 21. This is the single most commonly missed route among long-term TPS holders — and in many cases it leads to permanent residence.

It is not automatic and the details matter, particularly how you entered the country, which affects whether you can process your case inside the U.S. Get it assessed properly.

Other possibilities

A citizen or resident spouse; employment sponsorship; U or T visas for victims of certain crimes or trafficking; asylum, subject to deadline rules; and cancellation of removal for some long-residence cases with qualifying relatives.

Protect your history

Keep every TPS approval notice, EAD, tax return, and proof of residence. For nearly every alternative route, your documented history in the United States is the foundation of the case.
