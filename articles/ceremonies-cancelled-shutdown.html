Government shutdown cancels naturalization ceremonies nationwide

October 26, 2026Policy Update

From October into mid-November 2026, naturalization ceremonies were cancelled across the country as a result of the government shutdown — frequently at the last minute and without explanation.

In early October 2026, Congressman André Carson led 26 members of Congress in seeking answers from USCIS about repeated ceremony cancellations. In some cities, field offices cancelled ceremonies citing a "lack of candidates" while simultaneously holding pending applications awaiting action.

What this means if it happened to you

You are approved but not yet a citizen. You cannot get a U.S. passport, register to vote, or petition for relatives as a citizen until you take the Oath of Allegiance. People had family travel for ceremonies that were cancelled the day before.

Practical steps

Keep every notice
Including the cancellation. It documents where your case stands.
Do not let status documents lapse
If your green card expires while you wait for a ceremony, renew it. Approval is not a substitute for valid documents.
Contact your congressional representative's office
This is precisely the casework they do, at no charge, and they were already engaged on this issue.
Watch your mail and your online case status closely
For a rescheduled date, and keep your address current.
Be careful about travel
A rescheduled notice can arrive with little warning, and missing a ceremony creates further delay.
