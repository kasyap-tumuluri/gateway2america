USCIS reframes adjustment of status as "extraordinary relief"

May 26, 2026Policy Update

On May 21, 2026, USCIS issued Policy Memorandum PM-602-0199, recharacterising adjustment of status as a matter of discretion and "administrative grace" — an extraordinary relief that lets applicants skip the ordinary consular visa process.

What is actually changing

Adjustment of status is how someone already inside the United States applies for a green card without leaving (Form I-485). The alternative is consular processing: applying at a U.S. consulate abroad.

The memo directs officers to treat consular processing as the expected route, and adjustment as a discretionary exception that the applicant must affirmatively justify. Officers weigh a totality of circumstances: immigration history, status or parole violations, unauthorized employment, fraud or false testimony, failure to depart after a temporary admission, family ties, moral character, and humanitarian factors.

As practitioners have described it, the default shifted from "approve unless there is a reason not to" toward requiring extraordinary circumstances.

What it does not do

Importantly, the memo does not prohibit filing. People who are eligible for an immigrant visa may still apply for adjustment of status.

Why this matters so much

Leaving the United States is not a neutral alternative. For people who have accrued unlawful presence, departing can trigger three- and ten-year bars on returning. "Just go process abroad" can mean years of separation from family.

If you have a pending or planned I-485, this is a conversation to have with an immigration attorney before taking any action — especially before travelling.
