Nonprofits can again register voters at naturalization ceremonies

September 8, 2026Policy Update

USCIS rescinded its August 29, 2025 policy and reinstated guidance (PA-2026-11) in the Policy Manual on August 12, 2026, permitting nonprofit and nonpartisan organizations to participate in administrative naturalization ceremonies by distributing and collecting voter registration applications for new citizens.

The 2025 policy had limited voter registration services at ceremonies to state and local election officials. On October 16, 2026, a court permanently voided that ban, and the government waived its appeal — making the restoration durable rather than discretionary.

Why the ceremony is the right moment

Registering immediately after the oath has long been standard practice, for a practical reason: the people in that room have just become eligible to vote, they are all together, and the barrier to registering is at its lowest it will ever be. Registration rates drop sharply once everyone goes home.

For organizations

NGOs wishing to provide voter registration services must submit a written request to the appropriate local USCIS office at least 60 days before the ceremony. Plan well ahead.

For new citizens

Congratulations — take the oath first, then register. If nobody is offering registration at your ceremony, you can register through your state election office, at the DMV, or by mail. Deadlines vary by state, so check yours.

Guidance was also updated to clarify that the Oath of Allegiance is the final step of naturalization, and to add background on the oath's history.
