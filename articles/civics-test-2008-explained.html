The civics test, explained: what you are actually asked

January 13, 2025How It Works

The civics test is the part of naturalization people fear most, and it is the part most within your control, largely because the questions are published in advance and there are no surprises waiting for you.

The format

Under the version in use for applications filed before October 20, 2025, the officer asks up to 10 questions drawn from a public list of 100. You need 6 correct to pass, and the officer stops asking as soon as you have got there.

The test is spoken rather than written, so the officer asks and you answer out loud. There is no need to answer in full sentences. "Ninety-nine" is a fine answer to how many senators there are.

How to study

USCIS publishes the full question list, official answers, and free study materials — flash cards, audio, and practice tests. Use the official answers rather than your own phrasing, because some questions have several acceptable answers and the official list tells you which count.

A few answers change with elections and appointments: your state's senators, your representative, the Speaker of the House, the President and Vice President, your governor, your state capital. Check those close to your interview date.

If you do not pass

You get a second attempt. USCIS reschedules you, normally within 60 to 90 days, and you are retested only on the portion you failed.
