Neighborhood investigations formally added to the Policy Manual

August 18, 2026Policy Update

On August 25, 2026, USCIS issued a policy alert on Investigations and Examinations for Naturalization Eligibility, formally incorporating neighborhood investigations into the Policy Manual.

This follows the August 2025 memorandum that revived the authority under INA section 335(a), dormant since 1991. Moving it into the Policy Manual makes it settled operating guidance rather than a memo.

The guidance covers investigation of an applicant's conduct, residence, and reputation in the community.

What to actually do about it

The protective step is the same one that protects you against everything else in this process: make sure your application is accurate, and be able to prove it. An investigation only creates a problem if what it finds differs from what you wrote.

Corroborate your N-400
For each address and employer you listed, be able to produce a lease, a utility bill, pay stubs, or a tax document covering those dates.
Fix discrepancies before the interview
Not during it. If a date is wrong, correct it yourself.
Nobody has to talk to an investigator
Your neighbours, landlord, and employer may decline. That is their right and it is not evidence against you.
Check credentials
Real officials carry them. No legitimate officer will demand payment, threaten you, or ask for your password. This authority is an obvious cover story for scams.

USCIS has indicated these investigations will not occur in every case. If one happens in yours and you are uncertain how to respond, that is the point to consult an immigration attorney.
