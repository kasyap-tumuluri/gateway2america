The new public charge rule is now in effect

September 21, 2026Policy Update

The new public charge framework took effect on September 18, 2026. USCIS issued Policy Manual guidance on August 18, 2026 explaining how officers should apply it.

The two things to know

1. Your filing date decides your rule. Applications filed before September 18, 2026 are generally reviewed under the 2022 rule. Applications filed on or after that date fall under the new framework, in which officers are no longer limited to cash assistance and long-term institutionalization but weigh all pertinent facts case by case.

2. When you received a benefit matters. Benefits received before September 18, 2026 are assessed under the narrower standard — only cash assistance for income maintenance and long-term institutionalization count. Benefits received on or after that date may be considered more broadly, including means-tested benefits.

Who is not subject to public charge at all

This is the part most worth knowing, because fear here is widespread and often misplaced. Public charge rules do not apply to many categories, including refugees, asylees, VAWA self-petitioners, certain U and T visa holders, and others. If you are in one of these categories, benefit use does not create a public charge problem for you.

Please get advice before dropping benefits

Every time this rule changes, families disenroll from food assistance and medical coverage they are lawfully entitled to, including coverage for U.S. citizen children — whose benefits are generally not attributed to the parent anyway.

Before giving up any benefit, talk to a nonprofit legal provider or a benefits counsellor about your specific situation. Going without food or medical care to protect a case that was never at risk is a real and preventable harm.
