Supreme Court upholds birthright citizenship, 6–3

July 2, 2026Court Ruling

On June 30, 2026, the Supreme Court struck down Executive Order 14160 by a vote of 6–3. The order had sought to deny citizenship to U.S.-born children of undocumented parents.

The Court held that the order contradicted the plain language of the Fourteenth Amendment, and confirmed that a president cannot change constitutional rights through an executive order alone.

What this settles

Birthright citizenship remains in effect nationwide, exactly as it has for more than a century. If you are born on United States soil, you are a United States citizen. That is constitutional text, now reaffirmed by the Court.

This is about as clear an answer as this area of law produces, and it closes a question that caused real fear for a year and a half — particularly for parents who were pregnant when the order was signed.

If you have a U.S.-born child

Nothing unusual is required. The state or county birth certificate is the proof of citizenship. You can apply for a U.S. passport for your child in the normal way, and a passport is useful documentary proof of citizenship to have on hand.

A warning

Moments like this generate scams. Nobody needs to pay anyone to "confirm", "restore", or "protect" a U.S.-born child's citizenship. There is no special registration, no fee, and no application. If someone tells you otherwise, they are attempting to defraud you.
