DACA after the Fifth Circuit: renewals continue, new applications stall

February 17, 2025Court Ruling

On January 17, 2025, the Fifth Circuit Court of Appeals ruled that parts of DACA are unlawful. The decision left current recipients in place while litigation continues.

Where things stand

If you have DACA
Your status and work authorization continue. Renewals are being accepted and processed nationwide, including in Texas.
If you have never had DACA
You may submit a first-time application, but USCIS will not process it at this time. It sits pending.

What to do if you have DACA

Renew early. File in the window USCIS recommends — roughly 120 to 150 days before your expiration — and do not let it lapse. A gap in DACA is a gap in work authorization, and in some states a gap in your driver's license.

Keep copies of every approval notice you have ever received. Build a document file now: proof of continuous presence, school records, employment records. If the program's future changes, the people who can establish their history fastest will be best positioned.

DACA has been in and out of court for over a decade now, with decisions getting stayed, reversed, and narrowed along the way. Before acting on anything you read — here or anywhere — check the current status with a legal-aid organization.
