USCIS issues guidance on when it refers citizenship revocation cases

September 28, 2026Policy Update

On September 14, 2026, USCIS published policy alert PA-2026-13, "Revocation of Naturalization Referrals," setting out the circumstances in which USCIS refers cases for revocation of naturalization and when such cases are prioritized for referral.

This builds on the June 2025 DOJ memo that made denaturalization a top enforcement priority, and follows reporting of internal guidance seeking 100–200 referrals per month from field offices for the remainder of FY2026.

What denaturalization requires

It is worth being clear about the limits, because the reporting has caused considerable anxiety among naturalized citizens:

  • It is a civil court action, decided by a federal judge. An agency cannot revoke your citizenship by itself.
  • The grounds are essentially about how citizenship was obtained — concealment of a material fact or willful misrepresentation during the process, or certain ineligibility that existed at the time.
  • It is not a mechanism for revoking citizenship over conduct after naturalization, or over speech or political views.
  • The government bears a heavy burden of proof.

What this means for you

If you naturalized and answered your application truthfully, this is not aimed at you. Referral guidance is not a prediction about your case.

The enduring lesson is for people still applying: accuracy on the N-400 protects you permanently. Disclose and explain rather than omit. A disclosed problem is usually survivable; a concealed one can follow you past the oath ceremony.

If you ever receive notice of a denaturalization action, get an attorney immediately.
