The N-400, explained section by section

November 12, 2024How It Works

Form N-400 is the Application for Naturalization. It is long, and some questions sound more sinister than they are. Here is the shape of it.

Parts 1–3: Who you are and why you qualify

Basic identity information, and the basis for your application — five years as a permanent resident, three years as the spouse of a citizen, military service, and so on. Picking the wrong basis is a common and costly error, so be sure which one applies to you.

Parts 4–8: Your life on paper

Addresses, employment, marital history, children, and every trip outside the United States in the relevant period. The travel section is where people struggle most. Start reconstructing it early from passport stamps and old tickets — you need dates, not approximations.

Part 9: The long list of questions

This is the part that worries people. It asks about criminal history, past immigration violations, tax filings, organizational memberships, and more. Two rules: answer honestly, and answer completely. A "yes" is very often survivable once you explain it, whereas a false "no" gets treated as fraud and can cost you citizenship later, even after it is granted.

If any answer here is complicated, this is the point to talk to an actual immigration attorney before you file.

Parts 10–18: Oath, signature, accommodations

Requests for disability accommodation, requests to modify the oath on religious grounds, and your signature.
