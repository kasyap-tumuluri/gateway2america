Some visas now issued for three months with a single entry

July 15, 2025Policy Update

Effective July 8, 2025, the State Department changed visa validity and entry rules so that F-1, J-1, H-1B and certain other visas issued to nationals of specified countries on or after that date are valid for three months with a single entry.

Why this is a bigger deal than it sounds

A visa is permission to apply for admission at the border, which is a different thing from the status you hold once you are already here. That distinction is largely what makes this survivable:

  • If you are already in the U.S. in valid status, your I-94 and your program documents — not the visa in your passport — govern how long you may stay. An expired visa does not make you out of status.
  • The problem arises when you leave. With a single-entry visa, departing means you need a new visa to return, which means a new consular appointment abroad, with current wait times and no guarantee.

Practical guidance

If you hold one of these visas, treat any international travel as a serious decision rather than a routine one. Students and scholars should talk to their designated school official or responsible officer before booking, not after. Check consular appointment waits in the country where you would reapply.

Keep your I-94 record, your I-20 or DS-2019, and your current program documents together and current.
