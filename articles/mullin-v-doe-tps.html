Mullin v. Doe: Supreme Court limits review of TPS terminations

July 10, 2026Court Ruling

In Mullin v. Doe, decided in June 2026, the Supreme Court ruled 6–3 that the administration may cancel Temporary Protected Status for Syria and Haiti.

The more far-reaching part of the decision is structural: the Court held that federal law generally bars judicial review of TPS designation and termination decisions. Constitutional challenges remain available.

Why this matters beyond Syria and Haiti

Until now, litigation has been the main brake on TPS terminations — the reason protections for several countries stayed in place well past their announced end dates. This decision substantially removes that brake.

Practically: if TPS for your country is terminated, a lawsuit is much less likely to pause it. Do not plan on litigation buying you time.

What to do if you hold TPS from any country

Pursue a durable status now
While you have work authorization and lawful presence. Do not wait for a termination announcement.
Get a full screening
From a nonprofit immigration program. The most commonly missed option among long-term TPS holders is a U.S. citizen child aged 21 or over petitioning for a parent.
Keep re-registering
In every registration window.
Preserve your documentation
Approvals, EADs, tax returns, proof of continuous residence.

Advocates have turned toward Congress, since legislation is now the clearer route to permanent protection for long-term TPS holders.
