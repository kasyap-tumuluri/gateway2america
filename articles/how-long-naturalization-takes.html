How long does naturalization take?

March 3, 2025How It Works

There is no single answer, and anyone who gives you a confident one is guessing. What you can know is the sequence, and which stages tend to stall.

The stages

  1. File the N-400. A receipt notice (I-797) follows within a few weeks. Keep it.
  2. Biometrics. Fingerprints and photograph at an application support center. Often reused if you have given them before.
  3. Background checks. The invisible stage, and the usual source of long unexplained silences.
  4. Interview and tests. An officer reviews your application with you and administers the English and civics tests.
  5. Decision. Granted, continued (more evidence or a retest), or denied.
  6. Oath ceremony. You are not a citizen until you take the oath. Sometimes same-day, often weeks or months later.

Checking your own case

USCIS publishes current processing times by form and field office, and your receipt number lets you check your specific case online. Use those rather than anecdotes — field offices differ enormously.

If your case passes well beyond the posted time for your office, there are escalation routes: a service request, the USCIS Ombudsman, and a congressional inquiry through your representative's office. Those offices do casework like this routinely and it is free.
