Denaturalization becomes a top Justice Department priority

June 18, 2025Policy Update

On June 11, 2025, the Justice Department's Civil Division issued an internal memorandum listing its enforcement priorities. Denaturalization — the civil process of revoking U.S. citizenship — was named one of five top priorities, with attorneys directed to pursue such cases in all instances permitted by law and supported by evidence.

What denaturalization is

It is a civil court action to undo a naturalization that was obtained unlawfully — typically through concealment of a material fact or willful misrepresentation during the application process. It is not a power to revoke citizenship over conduct that occurred after you naturalized, and it requires a federal court, not an agency decision.

Context worth keeping

Historically these cases were rare and concentrated on serious matters such as concealed war crimes. The memo appears to broaden the circumstances pursued, and reporting in December 2025 indicated internal guidance asking USCIS field offices to refer 100–200 cases per month to DOJ for the remainder of FY2026.

What it means for you

If you naturalized and answered your application honestly, this is not aimed at you. The lesson it reinforces is for people still applying: accuracy on the N-400 protects you permanently. A false answer is not a problem that ends at the oath ceremony.

Anyone who receives notice of a denaturalization action needs an attorney immediately. Citizenship is at stake, and these are federal court proceedings.
