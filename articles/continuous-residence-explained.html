What "continuous residence" actually means

October 22, 2024How It Works

To naturalize, most people must show continuous residence as a permanent resident — five years for most applicants, three years for spouses of U.S. citizens. People often assume this just means "I have had my green card that long." It means more than that.

Trips abroad

Continuous residence is about whether you kept the United States as your home. Long absences can break it:

Under six months
Generally no problem.
Six months to one year
USCIS may presume you broke continuous residence. You can rebut that presumption with evidence that you kept ties here: a home, a job, tax filings, family.
One year or more
This generally breaks continuous residence outright, and your clock restarts.

Physical presence is a separate test

This trips people up. Alongside continuous residence, you must also have been physically present in the U.S. for at least half the required period — 30 months out of five years, or 18 months out of three. You can satisfy continuous residence and still fail physical presence, or the reverse. Both have to be met.

Before a long trip

If you know you will be outside the country for a long stretch, look into a re-entry permit or, for certain employment situations, Form N-470. Plan before you go, not after you return.
