Guidance issued on medical exams for Afghan arrivals under Operation Allies Welcome

November 18, 2024Policy Update

On November 13, 2024, USCIS updated its Policy Manual to address immigration medical examinations completed by certain Afghan nationals who arrived in the United States through Operation Allies Welcome.

Many people who arrived through that effort received medical screening on arrival, as part of the resettlement process. The question this guidance answers is whether that screening counts toward the medical examination requirement when they later apply for permanent residence — or whether they have to pay for and repeat the whole exam.

The update sets out when the earlier examination can be accepted, which spares affected applicants a duplicate exam and its cost.

Who this affects

This is narrow. It matters if you are an Afghan national who arrived under Operation Allies Welcome and are now applying for adjustment of status. It does not change the medical exam requirement for anyone else.

If this describes you, it is worth confirming with a resettlement agency or an accredited representative whether your existing exam records satisfy the requirement before scheduling a new appointment. Many of the organizations that handled Operation Allies Welcome arrivals still provide free help with exactly this kind of question.
