Naturalization backlog tops 813,000 as waits reach 12.7 months

October 13, 2026Policy Update

The naturalization backlog reached 813,453 pending N-400 applications as of August 2026, with average processing time at 12.7 months — up from just over seven months at the start of 2026. About 63% of pending cases had been waiting more than six months.

Decisions fell steeply. USCIS approved 54,974 naturalization applications in the third quarter of FY2026, against 236,924 in the same quarter a year earlier. Roughly 71,000 applications were decided between April and June 2026, compared with 265,000 in the same period of 2025.

Why

Several causes compound: expanded screening and vetting requirements, adjudication holds through early 2026, the revival of neighborhood investigations, and the activation of the FBI's Next Generation Identification system, which required millions of archived fingerprints to be resubmitted and temporarily stopped adjudications.

What to do while you wait

Keep your address current with USCIS
The most common self-inflicted disaster in a long wait is a missed notice leading to denial for abandonment. Update it with USCIS directly.
Renew anything that expires
A pending N-400 does not extend your green card. If it expires while you wait, file the I-90.
Keep studying
A twelve-month wait is long enough to forget material you had learned.
Check your case and the posted times
For your field office, which vary enormously.
Escalate if you are beyond the posted time
A service request, the USCIS Ombudsman, and a congressional inquiry. All free.
