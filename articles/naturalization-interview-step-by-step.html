The naturalization interview, step by step

March 28, 2025How It Works

The interview is less an interrogation than a careful read-through of your own application. Knowing the shape of it removes most of the fear.

What to bring

  • Your interview appointment notice.
  • Green card and a state ID or passport.
  • Any document the notice specifically asks for.
  • Updates since filing: a new address, a new job, a marriage, new travel, any new encounter with law enforcement.

In the room

You will be sworn in to tell the truth. The officer then goes through your N-400 with you, section by section, confirming and updating. This is your chance to correct anything that has changed — take it.

The English and civics tests happen in the same appointment. There is no separate speaking test: your ability to speak and understand English is assessed across the whole conversation, which is why the officer's small talk is not only small talk.

At the end

You will usually get a form telling you the result: granted, continued, or denied. "Continued" most often means a missing document or a retest, not a refusal.

Two practical notes

Say "I don't understand, could you repeat that?" freely. It is not held against you, and guessing at a misheard question is worse. And if you do not know an answer about your own history, say you do not know rather than inventing one — false testimony is far more serious than a gap in memory.
