Court temporarily blocks the end of TPS for South Sudan

December 31, 2025Court Ruling

On December 30, 2025, a court temporarily blocked the federal government from ending Temporary Protected Status protections for South Sudan. Protections remain valid until further notice.

What "temporarily blocked" means

A court order pausing a termination is not a permanent guarantee. It preserves the status quo while a case is decided, and it can be narrowed, lifted on appeal, or replaced by a different outcome. Treat it as breathing room, not as a resolution.

What to do with the time

Keep re-registering
Whenever USCIS opens a registration period. Court protection does not excuse you from the paperwork, and missing a window is how people lose status they were entitled to keep.
Use the pause to find a durable option
Get a full screening from a nonprofit immigration program: family petitions, employment routes, asylum, U or T visas, cancellation of removal.
Keep your documents current
Approval notices, EADs, proof of continuous residence, tax filings.

Verify before you act

TPS litigation moved in both directions through 2025 and 2026, and a Supreme Court decision in 2026 significantly limited judicial review of TPS terminations. Confirm your country's current status on the official USCIS TPS page rather than relying on any article, including this one.
