Refugee admissions ceiling set at 7,500 — the lowest ever

November 5, 2025Policy Update

On October 31, 2025, a Presidential Determination set the annual ceiling for refugee admissions at 7,500 for fiscal year 2026 — the lowest in United States history.

For scale: since the Refugee Act of 1980 established the modern resettlement program, the annual ceiling has averaged roughly 90,700. The determination also stated that the limited slots would be allocated primarily to Afrikaners from South Africa.

What the ceiling is

It caps how many refugees may be admitted through the U.S. Refugee Admissions Program in a fiscal year — people identified and processed abroad, usually through UNHCR referral, then resettled here.

What it does not affect

This next distinction matters quite a lot: the ceiling does not limit asylum. Asylum is for people already in the United States or at a port of entry, it is a separate legal process, and it has no numerical cap. If you are here and fear returning to your country, the refugee ceiling does not close that door.

If you have a pending refugee case abroad, or family waiting on one, expect severe delays and talk to the resettlement agency handling the case about what the allocation means for you.

The FY2026 ceiling was later raised to 17,500 by an emergency determination in May 2026, which we covered separately.
