The 50/20, 55/15, and 65/20 rules: when you can skip the English test

December 9, 2025How It Works

If you are an older applicant who has held a green card for a long time, you may not have to take the test in English at all. These exemptions are under-used, often because nobody mentions them.

50/20

Age 50 or older at filing, and a permanent resident for 20 years or more. You are exempt from the English requirement. You still take the civics test, but in your language of choice, with an interpreter you bring.

55/15

Age 55 or older, permanent resident for 15 years or more. Same deal: no English requirement, civics in your own language.

65/20

Age 65 or older, permanent resident for 20 years or more. Exempt from English, and you take a reduced civics set — a smaller number of designated questions, in your own language.

How to use an exemption

Count carefully
Both the age and the years as a permanent resident are measured at the time of filing. Someone close to a threshold may benefit from waiting a few months.
Bring your own interpreter
Who must be capable of interpreting faithfully. USCIS does not supply one.
Note what is not exempted
The civics knowledge itself, and every other requirement — continuous residence, physical presence, good moral character, the oath.

If you are helping an older parent or grandparent apply, check these rules first. They change how you prepare entirely.
