Court strikes down ICE courthouse arrest policy nationwide

August 27, 2026Know Your Rights

A federal court struck down ICE's immigration-courthouse arrest policy nationwide, finding it arbitrary and capricious under the Administrative Procedure Act and restoring the pre-existing policies that barred courthouse arrests except in rare circumstances.

The policy at issue grew out of a May 2025 internal memo directing ICE trial attorneys to support deportation officers by moving to dismiss immigration cases, enabling arrests at the courthouse. Under the ruling, officers may not conduct civil immigration enforcement in or near immigration courthouses except in limited circumstances.

Why it mattered

Being arrested for attending your own hearing creates an impossible choice: appear and risk detention, or skip it and get an automatic removal order. People stopped showing up to proceedings they were legally required to attend.

Know your rights at any courthouse appearance

Always attend your hearing
Missing it generally produces an in-absentia removal order, which is far worse than any risk of attending.
You have the right to remain silent
You do not have to answer questions about where you were born, your immigration status, or how you entered.
Do not sign anything
Without talking to a lawyer — especially a document waiving your right to a hearing, such as a voluntary departure or stipulated removal form.
If detained
Do not run, do not resist, do not lie. Stay silent and ask for your attorney.

Preparing

Know your immigration history, your courthouse, and your judge. Appear remotely if that is permitted in your case. Bring the documents you need and leave the ones you do not. Have a written family plan — childcare, finances, important documents, phone numbers — in case the worst happens.
