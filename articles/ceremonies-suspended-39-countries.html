Oath ceremonies suspended and cases paused for applicants from 39 countries

January 28, 2026Policy Update

In January 2026, naturalization oath ceremonies were suspended and immigration case adjudication halted for applicants from 39 countries. Separately, ceremonies were cancelled in individual cities — Austin halted its ceremonies on January 23, 2026, one local instance of a national pattern.

Why the oath matters so much

You are not a U.S. citizen until you take the Oath of Allegiance. Someone whose application was approved but whose ceremony is cancelled is in a genuinely difficult position: approved, but not yet a citizen, and unable to get a passport, register to vote, or petition for relatives as a citizen.

If your ceremony was cancelled

Keep every notice
Including the cancelled appointment notice. It is evidence of where your case stands.
Do not let other documents lapse
If your green card or work authorization expires while you wait, renew it. Approval of naturalization is not a substitute for valid status documents.
Contact your congressional representative's office
They do immigration casework for constituents at no charge, and cancelled ceremonies are exactly the kind of problem they take up — 26 members of Congress formally questioned USCIS about these cancellations in October 2026.
Watch your mail closely
For a rescheduled notice.
