Marriage fraud indictments, and how to protect a real marriage case

February 17, 2026Know Your Rights

On February 10, 2026, USCIS assisted an investigation into a marriage fraud conspiracy that resulted in 11 indictments.

We cover this not as crime news but because enforcement of this kind raises scrutiny for everyone, and honest couples are the ones caught off guard by it.

What officers look for

The question in a marriage-based case is whether the marriage is bona fide — entered into for a shared life, not to obtain an immigration benefit. Officers assess that through documentation and, where there are doubts, through separate interviews of each spouse.

Documenting a real marriage

Real marriages sometimes look thin on paper, especially for couples who keep finances separate or live with family. Build the record deliberately:

  • Joint lease or mortgage, joint utility accounts.
  • Joint bank accounts, and each of you named on the other's insurance, retirement beneficiary forms, and emergency contacts.
  • Joint tax returns.
  • Dated photographs across time and with each other's families.
  • Travel records together; correspondence during separations.
  • Affidavits from people who know you both.

If you are a victim rather than a participant

If someone married you in order to use your status, or if you are experiencing abuse in a marriage your immigration case depends on, there are specific protections — including VAWA self-petitions, which let you file without your spouse's knowledge or involvement. Talk to a legal-aid organization or the National Domestic Violence Hotline.
