The English requirement: reading, writing, and the part nobody tells you about

August 11, 2025How It Works

The English requirement has three parts, and the third is the one that surprises people.

Reading

You read one sentence aloud correctly, from up to three attempts. The vocabulary comes from a published list.

Writing

You write one sentence correctly, dictated by the officer, from up to three attempts. Again, a published vocabulary list. Spelling errors are tolerated if the meaning is clear.

Speaking and understanding — the hidden part

There is no separate speaking test. Your ability to speak and understand English is assessed throughout the entire interview — while the officer reviews your application, asks about your background, and administers the civics questions. The whole conversation is the test.

This is why practising only the civics answers is not enough. Practise talking about your own life in English: where you work, who lives with you, when you travelled and why.

The standard is lower than people fear

What is being tested here is basic English rather than fluency, so an accent really is not a problem. Asking the officer to repeat something is fine too, and honestly far better than guessing at what they said.

Exemptions

You may be exempt from English based on age and time as a permanent resident — the 50/20, 55/15, and 65/20 rules — or on disability grounds via Form N-648. We cover those separately.
