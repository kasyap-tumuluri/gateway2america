Proposed rule would raise the citizenship fee to $1,330 and end fee waivers

June 26, 2026Policy Update

DHS published a notice of proposed rulemaking on June 22, 2026 that would substantially raise filing fees for naturalization.

What is proposed

FormNowProposed
N-400, paper$760$1,330
N-400, online$710$1,280
N-336 appeal, online$780$1,425
N-336 appeal, mail$830$1,475

That is roughly a 75% increase on the N-400. The proposal would also eliminate the reduced fee option and fee waivers for the N-400 — which is, for many families, the more consequential change.

It is not in effect

This is a proposed rule. A 60-day public comment period ran from June 22, 2026, and current fees remain in effect until a final rule is published and takes effect. Do not pay $1,330 because you read that it is the new price. Check the USCIS fee page the day you file.

What to do if you are eligible now

If you already qualify to naturalize, there is a straightforward argument for not waiting. Filing under current fees — and under the current reduced-fee and waiver rules, if you qualify for them — locks in today's cost.

If cost is what has been stopping you, look hard at the reduced fee and waiver options while they exist. The 400%-of-poverty-guidelines threshold for the $380 reduced fee reaches further into working-household income than most people expect.

Rules can still change quite a bit before they become final, so we will post again once there is an actual final rule.
