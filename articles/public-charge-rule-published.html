New public charge rule published, effective September 18

July 27, 2026Policy Update

A final rule published in the Federal Register on July 20, 2026 rescinds the 2022 public charge rule, effective September 18, 2026.

What public charge means

Most people applying for a green card must show they are not likely to become a public charge — primarily dependent on government support. How narrowly that is assessed has swung repeatedly between administrations.

What changes

Under the 2022 rule, officers were limited to considering public cash assistance for income maintenance and long-term institutionalization at government expense. From September 18, 2026, officers are directed to weigh all pertinent facts, case by case.

Which rule applies to you

  • Filed before September 18, 2026 → generally reviewed under the 2022 rule.
  • Filed on or after → the new framework.

Benefits: timing matters

This distinction is important and easy to miss:

  • Benefits received before September 18, 2026 — only cash assistance for income maintenance and long-term institutionalization count against you.
  • Benefits received on or after — any means-tested benefit may be considered.

Please do not drop benefits out of fear

Many benefits have never counted, and many people are not subject to public charge rules at all — including refugees, asylees, VAWA self-petitioners, and certain other categories. Benefits used by your children are generally not attributed to you.

Families disenrolling from food and medical assistance they are lawfully entitled to, out of fear, is a recurring and avoidable harm. Get advice about your specific situation before giving anything up.
