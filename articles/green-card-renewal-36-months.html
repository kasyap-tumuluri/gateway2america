Your green card renewal receipt now extends your card for 36 months

October 14, 2024Policy Update

If you have filed to renew your green card and the card itself expired while you were waiting, you are not out of status and you are not undocumented. Your receipt notice carries you.

USCIS raised the automatic extension of green card validity from 24 months to 36 months. Receipt notices reflecting the longer window started going out on September 10, 2024.

How it works

When you file Form I-90 (Application to Replace Permanent Resident Card) with the correct fee, USCIS sends you a receipt notice called Form I-797. That notice now says your green card is extended for 36 months past the expiration date printed on the card.

To use it, carry both documents together — the expired green card and the I-797 receipt notice. On their own, neither one proves what you need it to prove. Together they establish your permanent resident status for work authorization and for travel.

Why this matters

Renewal processing has been slow enough that the old 24-month extension was running out before cards arrived, leaving people unable to prove status at a new job or on re-entry. The longer window is a practical fix.

If your receipt notice is older and shows only a 24-month extension, you can ask USCIS for an updated notice.
