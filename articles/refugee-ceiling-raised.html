Refugee ceiling raised from 7,500 to 17,500

June 2, 2026Policy Update

On May 21, 2026, an Emergency Presidential Determination raised the fiscal year 2026 refugee admissions ceiling from 7,500 to 17,500. The additional admissions were allocated to Afrikaners from South Africa.

Context

The original FY2026 ceiling of 7,500, set in October 2025, was the lowest in U.S. history — against a historic average of roughly 90,700 since the Refugee Act of 1980. The revised figure of 17,500 remains far below that average.

The allocation of the increase to a single population is itself notable, since the refugee program has historically been structured around regional allocations responding to displacement worldwide.

What this means in practice

If you have a refugee case pending abroad, or family waiting on one, the additional slots are unlikely to help unless your case falls within the designated allocation. Talk to the resettlement agency handling the case about where it actually stands.

The distinction to hold onto

The refugee ceiling governs refugee admissions from abroad. It does not cap asylum, which is for people already in the United States or at a port of entry and has no numerical limit.

If you are here and fear returning to your country, the ceiling does not apply to you. Asylum has its own rules — including a general one-year filing deadline from arrival, with exceptions — and it is worth getting screened by a nonprofit legal provider rather than assuming you are out of options.
