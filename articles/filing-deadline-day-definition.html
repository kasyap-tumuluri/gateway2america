USCIS clarifies how "day" is counted for filing deadlines

October 29, 2026Policy Update

USCIS issued policy guidance, effective October 23, 2026, clarifying how it applies the regulatory definition of "day" to filing deadlines.

This sounds like housekeeping. It is not, if you are the person who filed one day late.

Why deadline counting matters so much

Immigration deadlines are generally unforgiving, and several carry consequences that are difficult or impossible to undo:

Responses to requests for evidence
Late means your case is decided on the record as it stands — usually a denial.
Appeals and motions
Which have short windows.
The asylum one-year filing deadline
From arrival.
The I-730 two-year deadline
For follow-to-join relatives.
TPS re-registration periods

Practical rules that protect you

Read the deadline on your own notice
Rather than relying on a general rule. The notice governs.
File early, not on the last day
Aim for a week ahead. This single habit prevents most deadline disasters.
Keep proof of filing
Tracked, signed-for mail, or the online confirmation and receipt number.
Watch out for weekends and federal holidays
Which is exactly the ambiguity this guidance addresses.
Do not wait for complete evidence
If a deadline is near. File what you have with an explanation rather than missing the date.
