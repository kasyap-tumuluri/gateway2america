The document file to build before you file anything

June 15, 2026How It Works

Whatever route you are on, the same records keep being requested. Building this file once — before you need it — is the most useful preparation you can do.

Identity and status

  • Passports, including expired ones. Every page with a stamp matters.
  • Green card, front and back. Any prior work permits.
  • Your A-number, and every USCIS receipt number you have ever had.
  • Every notice USCIS has ever sent you — receipts, approvals, denials, requests for evidence. Keep the originals, scan everything.
  • Birth certificate, with a certified translation if it is not in English.
  • Marriage certificates; divorce decrees or death certificates ending prior marriages.

Residence and work history

  • Every address for the last five years, with exact move-in and move-out dates.
  • Every employer for the last five years, with addresses and dates.
  • Tax returns and transcripts. The IRS provides transcripts free.
  • Leases, mortgage statements, utility bills.

Travel

This is where people lose weeks. Reconstruct every trip outside the United States with departure and return dates. Use passport stamps, old boarding passes, email confirmations, and your I-94 travel history, which is available free online from CBP.

Anything involving law enforcement

Certified dispositions for every arrest, charge, or citation — even if dismissed, expunged, or resolved without a conviction. "It was dropped" is not enough; you need the court record.

Two habits

Scan everything and keep a backup somewhere you can reach from anywhere. And keep a single running timeline document of addresses, jobs, and trips. Update it as life happens, so you are never reconstructing five years from memory.

Where this information comes from

  • IRS — Get Transcript
